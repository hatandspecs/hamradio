Fourteen Ferrite Chokes Wound and Measured, and Why I Could Not Predict Any of Them

Published on: November 14, 2026  · 

Where this stands. Fourteen four-core common-mode chokes wound on Fair-Rite sleeves with RG-316, each swept 1 to 30 MHz, plus eighteen single-sleeve sweeps behind them and two re-winds with tidier leads. Fourteen built assemblies is enough to test a design model instead of trusting one, and that test is most of what this article records.

Ten of the fourteen hold 5 kilohms of resistance on every band from 80 m through 10 m. The best holds 8,587 ohms and stays resistance-dominant throughout. It is four separate single-core sections: a mix-75 sleeve at eight turns and a mix 31 at eight for the low end, a six-turn mix 31 and a five-turn mix 43 for the high end.

Re-dressing a finished build with tidier leads adds 22 to 52 percent at 10 m and nothing at all below 20 m. That is measured twice, on two different assemblies, and it is the clearest single result in this work: the only thing here that improved a choke rather than ruling something out.

No model in this work predicted any of it. Five independent prediction routes were tried against the measured builds and all five failed by 23 to 47 percent on the one number a build is chosen by. Three chokes picked by the best of those models came in 24, 31 and 75 percent below prediction. The second half of this article is the record of that failure, because it turned out to be more useful than the predictions were.

This article is RG-316 only. RG-400 does not work in any four-core design on these cores, because not enough turns fit through the bore to reach the resistance wanted, so everything below is RG-316.

The criterion. At least 5 kilohms of resistance on every band, and resistance rather than reactance. Common-mode current falls as the choke’s impedance rises against the antenna system’s own common-mode impedance, which for a wire antenna and feedline sits in the hundreds of ohms to low thousands; a choke an order of magnitude above that stops being the limit. Resistance matters separately because a choke that is mostly reactance stores energy and hands it back, and moves the resonance of whatever it is attached to. A choke is resistance-dominant where R is at least the magnitude of X, which is R/|Z| at or above 0.707. Every figure here is the real part.

How they were measured

The series-through fixture. The choke under test clips between the two posts, and the bare wire alongside is the reference.

A NanoVNA-F V2, SOLT calibrated, series-through fixture, 1 to 30 MHz at 301 points, with Zcm = 2·Z0·(1 − S21)/S21.

The fixture residual is subtracted here, and divided out in the toroid choke article, and both are right. How to remove a reference depends on what the reference is. For a series element S21 = 2·Z0/(2·Z0 + Z), and two series elements compose as Z1 + Z2, which is not multiplicative in S21, so division returns Z/(1 + Za/2·Z0) rather than Z − Za. Division is exact instead for a delay, which contributes phase at unit magnitude.

The discriminator is |S21|: a delay holds it at 1, a lumped reactance drops it as frequency rises. Fitting both models to each reference over 8 to 29 MHz settles it. The compact short used for the single toroid choke, fixture ends brought together, is a 0.85 ns delay and fits that model 2.3 times better, so that article divides. The long wire spanning this build’s geometry is a 453 nH lumped inductance and fits that model 1.9 times better, so this one subtracts. An earlier version of this article divided a lumped reference, which was wrong for this fixture. choke_s2p.py now runs the fit and picks on its own, printing which it chose.

Which reference matters, and one reference covers all of them. The assembly geometry’s short measures 455 nH of residual inductance against 289 nH at the single-sleeve geometry, so sweep SJ000046 and not SJ000026 is the correct subtraction for every choke above. Chokes 1 to 11 were swept the same day in the same fixture at the same lead distances, and choke 2 re-dressed the following day against that same reference with the fixture and calibration untouched. Chokes 12 and 13 came eight days later, which is a weaker claim on the same reference and is why it was checked: at these impedances subtracting SJ000046 and using no reference at all agree within 0.3 percent on every band, 453 nH being 81 ohms at 28.4 MHz against 8,000 of winding.

The fixture’s ceiling runs 2.1 MΩ at 160 m to 173 kΩ at 10 m. Every reading below is 0 to 9 percent of that ceiling, so nothing here is the fixture measuring itself.

Watch out. No measured number in this article comes from above a winding’s own resonance. The two de-embedding conventions differ by a factor of 40 there: for mix 31 at eight turns at 28.4 MHz, subtraction gives 2,283 ohms and division gives 54 for the same sweep, and the material model says 390. Neither convention is usable past resonance.

The two halves of the criterion disagree for every build but four. Choke 11 has a high worst-band resistance, 7,369 ohms, but its R/|Z| falls to 0.594 at 10 m, so most of what it presents there is reactance it will hand back. Ten chokes clear 5 kilohms on every band from 80 m to 10 m. Only four stay resistance-dominant while doing it: 1, 2, 13 and 14.

Choke 12 re-dressed is the near miss worth naming. It reaches 8,925 ohms on its worst band, more than anything else here, and fails dominance at 10 m by 44 ohms: R/|Z| 0.705 against the 0.707 line. The same assembly with its mix-43 section cut from six turns to five is choke 13, which gives up 338 ohms of worst-band resistance and clears dominance by a wide margin.

The best measuring configurations

Choke 13 is the best choke wound. #75@8T + #31@8T + #31@6T + #43@5T, 8,587 ohms on its worst band from 80 m to 10 m, R/|Z| never below 0.782, and resistance-dominant on all nine bands from 160 m up. Its worst band sits 1,729 ohms above the next build that also stays resistive, choke 2 re-dressed. That build is the better one at 10 m alone, 10,166 ohms against 8,587; 80 m is what sets its score.

Choke 14 is second on the number and is still not the one to build. #75@8T + #31@8T + #31@6T + #31@5T, 8,094 ohms worst with R/|Z| never below 0.725, so it meets both halves and ranks second of the fourteen. What the mix change actually does is move 9 to 13 percent of resistance into 40 through 17 m, where choke 13 already held three times the criterion, and take 5 and 6 percent off 12 and 10 m, where the margin is the thin one. Its dominance at 10 m is the reason to pass: 0.725 at band center and 0.710 across the whole band, against choke 13’s 0.782 and 0.763, and choke 12 re-dressed failed the same line by 0.002. Gains in the middle of the range are not worth paying for at the top, because the middle was never where any of these builds was in trouble.

The shaded span stops just short of the right edge, at 29.6 MHz, where choke 13’s runs the full sweep. That gap is the whole difference between the two builds: everything choke 14 gains it gains in the broad flat top between 7 and 15 MHz, and what it gives up is the last half megahertz of 10 m. The single-sample excursion at 13.9 MHz is noise, 900 ohms above its neighbors on no band.

Choke 2 re-dressed is third and holds the quality record. 6,858 ohms worst with R/|Z| never below 0.888, which is a wider dominance margin than either choke 13 or choke 14 manages. If the only thing that matters is that nothing anywhere is reactive, it is still the cleanest sweep here.

Choke 1 is fourth and is the flattest. #75@8T + #75@8T + #43@5T + #43@5T, 6,307 ohms worst. Its weak band is 80 m rather than 10 m, which makes it the flattest of the four across the high bands, and it reaches 12,286 ohms at 17 m.

Chokes 11 and 12 win on raw resistance and lose on quality. Worth building if the only number that matters is worst-band R, and worth skipping otherwise. Choke 12 re-dressed misses dominance at 10 m by 44 ohms.

What separates the winners is the high-end section, and what it wants is few turns. All four chokes that meet the full criterion carry five turns there, and no build here uses fewer. Every build whose high-end section carries six or eight turns gives up 10 m, including choke 12, which is choke 13 with that one section at six turns instead of five. The mix in that slot is a trade, not a requirement: choke 14 reaches the criterion with mix 31 there and no mix 43 anywhere in it, buying the middle of the range and paying for it at the top.

Nothing below 20 m moved, and everything above it did, both times. That is the signature of stray capacitance and nothing else: capacitance is invisible where the reactance it shunts is small, and it sets the ceiling where that reactance is large. The fits agree, choke 12’s stray falling from 0.45 to 0.38 pF across the re-dress. The lead dressing was carrying capacitance across the winding, and taking it out raised the ceiling the top four bands were sitting under.

Three consequences for the build:

  • Dressing is worth 22 to 52 percent at 10 m, measured on two different assemblies, and costs nothing but care.
  • It moved choke 2’s limiting band from 10 m to 80 m. That choke is no longer short of impedance at the top; it is short at the bottom, where only more turns or more permeability will help. Its worst R/|Z| improved from 0.76 to 0.89.
  • It did not win 10 m for choke 12, which lands at R/|Z| 0.705 against the 0.707 line. Dominance is conductance against susceptance, and a tidier winding dissipates less as well as storing less, so Rp rose from 16,084 to 17,938 ohms at 28.4 MHz while the susceptance fell. Both sides of the ratio moved together. Cutting stray capacitance cannot buy dominance on its own.

This also measures the single-sleeve capacitance figures from the other direction. Those were taken with each sleeve’s loops standing proud and alligator clips centimeters apart, and the article calls them an upper bound. Re-dressing an assembly and watching only the top four bands move is the same statement made on a built choke: lead geometry, not the ferrite, sets what the high bands do.

Watch out. Choke 12 re-dressed reads 4,063 ohm at 160 m, which is a bad sample and not a feature: 1.8 and 1.9 MHz dip two to three kilohms below their neighbors while the same assembly before and after runs smoothly through. That cell is left out of the table above. All three of the 2026-11-22 sweeps carry |S11| > 1 over 1.0 to 2.26 MHz, which does not touch the series-through R and X but is a reason not to lean on 160 m.

The design rule the measurements give

Every choke here is limited at either 10 m or 160 m, and turns trade one against the other. The one-at-a-time builds measure the trade directly, each comparison being one choke against one choke:

  • Choke 8 to choke 9, high-end section #43@6T to #43@8T: 10 m rises from 4,378 to 6,282 ohms, and the limit moves to 160 m at 5,931. But R/|Z| at 10 m falls from 0.58 to 0.55, so the extra resistance arrives with more reactance beside it. This is the one comparison in the set where resistance and dominance move in opposite directions, and it is why the rule below is stated on dominance rather than on resistance.
  • Choke 10 to choke 11, #43@6T to #43@5T at nine turns of mix 75: 10 m rises from 4,256 to 7,369 ohms and R/|Z| from 0.55 to 0.59, and again the limit moves low, to 6,651 ohms at 160 m.
  • Resistance at 10 m is not monotonic in mix-43 turns; dominance is. Six turns is the worst case for resistance, and going either way from it helps, which is what the first two bullets say between them. Dominance has no such turn. All three one-at-a-time pairs agree that taking turns off the mix-43 section improves it: 0.58 at six against 0.55 at eight, 0.59 at five against 0.55 at six, 0.78 at five against 0.67 at six. The bases differ, so only the direction within each pair carries, and all three point the same way.
  • Choke 7 to choke 8, replacing a #75@8T section with #31@6T: 10 m falls by 1,260 ohms. The mix-75 sections are load-bearing at the top of the range, not only at the bottom, which was not expected.
  • Choke 8 to choke 10, #75@8T to #75@9T: nothing gained at 10 m, while 30 m rises from 16,109 to 35,171 ohms. The ninth turn moves impedance to a band that already had a large surplus.
  • Choke 12 to choke 13, high-end section #43@6T to #43@5T: 10 m rises from 7,312 to 8,587 ohms and R/|Z| from 0.674 to 0.782, while 30 m falls from 18,187 to 14,655. The clearest version of the trade in the set, and the only one that converts a build that fails 10 m into one that passes it.
  • Choke 13 to choke 14, the high-end section’s mix alone, #43@5T to #31@5T: 40 through 17 m rise 9 to 13 percent, 12 and 10 m fall 5 and 6 percent, and R/|Z| at 10 m drops from 0.782 to 0.725. The only change of mix in the set made at a fixed turn count, which is what separates what the mix does from what the turns do. Mix 43 in that slot is worth about half a kilohm at 10 m and costs about two in the middle.

Turns raise low-frequency impedance and lower the section’s own resonance, so turns bought at the top are paid for at the bottom. A four-core choke covering 80 through 10 m wants its sections deliberately mismatched: many turns of high-permeability mix for the bottom, few turns of a lower-permeability mix for the top, and nothing in between trying to do both. That is what chokes 1, 2 and 13 are.

The high-end section decides 10 m, and the mechanism is loss rather than capacitance. Past self-resonance an assembly is a parallel R-L-C, and R >= |X| reduces to 1/Rp >= wC - 1/(wL), conductance against susceptance. Choke 13’s five-turn mix 43 drops Rp at 28.4 MHz to 14,045 ohms where choke 12 re-dressed sits at 17,938, raising the conductance 28 percent while the susceptance barely moves. The bench sweeps bracket that section without characterizing it: a mix-43 sleeve at four turns reads 2,211 ohms at 28.4 MHz and is still climbing, at six turns it has fallen to 678 ohms against 3,085 of reactance, and five turns was never swept on its own. Choke 14 reads the same mechanism backwards. Its mix-31 section leaves Rp at 28.4 MHz at 15,404 ohms where choke 13’s mix 43 pulls it down to 14,045, so it dissipates less at the top and gives up the dominance that the loss was buying, 0.725 against 0.782. Dominance at the top is bought with loss, not with geometry, which is the opposite of what the re-dressing result above suggests and is why both measurements were needed.

Watch out. The #43@5T section that chokes 1, 2 and 13 all depend on is never swept on its own, so that part of this rule rests on the assemblies rather than on any characterization of the part. The scatter between two nominally identical windings is still unknown, because the one repeat taken here, choke 12 against choke 12 re-dressed, deliberately changed the dressing and so bounds that rather than the scatter. Choke 11 and choke 12 sit 57 ohms apart on worst-band resistance, which this article cannot call a difference.

Fair-Rite’s data is not wrong; it is measured on a specified toroid and does not transfer intact to a 26 mm sleeve.

Mix 75 cannot be characterized by either dataset. Every mix-75 winding, down to one turn, has its impedance maximum at or below the 1 MHz start of the sweep. The data cannot separate two readings: either the windings are past an LC resonance below 1 MHz, or mix 75’s loss permeability peaks below 1 MHz as a material property and the windings are fine. The second is physically expected for a high-permeability manganese-zinc ferrite, and a one-turn mix-75 sleeve calculates to resonate near 107 MHz. But the measured reactance crosses negative at 1.1 to 1.4 MHz, which is the signature of the first, and at 70 to 100 ohms that sign is fixture noise. A sweep starting at 100 kHz would settle it. Earlier passes assumed a flat 0.41 for mix 75 and every build they produced is withdrawn.

Why the naive models did not work

Not because sections interact. That was the hypothesis the failures suggested, and it is wrong. Eleven assemblies built from nine distinct section types is an over-determined system, so one impedance can be solved for each section type and checked against every assembly at once. That solve closes:

Residual 1% 3% 6% 8% 6% 6% 5% 6% 8%

Sections add in series, to within 1 to 8 percent. One consistent value per section reproduces eleven different chokes. Adjacent sleeves on a shared run are not coupling in any way that matters, and Z_total = ΣZ_section is sound.

It is the per-section impedances that are wrong, and they came from the bench. The single-sleeve sweeps are the only source of them, and they were taken at a looser lead dressing than any assembly: loops standing proud, clips centimeters apart, 289 nH of fixture residual against the assembly geometry’s 455. That geometry adds capacitance, which drops the resonance and takes the middle and upper bands with it. So every capacitance figure derived from a single sleeve, 0.21 to 0.33 pF per turn, is an upper bound, and the assembled value is lower, in the direction of the commercial 0.15 to 0.28. Recalibrating the capacitance in either direction bottoms out at 23 percent mean error and never reproduces the shape: the model gives flat responses where the real chokes have strong mid-band peaks.

And the right per-section values cannot be recovered from these eleven chokes. Solving a section library from the assemblies fits them well and then fails to predict. Holding each choke out, fitting the rest, and predicting the one held out misses by 27 percent on average, from −60 to +59 percent, and several sections solve to negative resistance above 20 m. The cause is in the experiment rather than the arithmetic: #75@8T appears in six of the seven cleanest builds, so the columns are nearly collinear and any division of impedance between sections that sums correctly will fit. The sum is measured. The split is not.

Adding one end-to-end stray capacitance across the whole assembly removes the negative values at 0.75 to 1.0 pF for no loss of fit, which is a reason to prefer that small value on parsimony. It is not a measurement of one: the residual falls monotonically as that capacitance is raised with no interior minimum, by 5 pF the solved sections decay as 1/f, meaning the parameter is absorbing signal rather than explaining it, and out-of-sample error gets worse at 47 percent.

What would settle it is sweeping each candidate section alone, in the assembly fixture, at the assembly lead dressing. Five sweeps. Series summation is measured sound, so correct per-section impedances are the only missing ingredient, and the existing single-sleeve set cannot supply them.

What this does not establish

No stacked section has ever been swept. All fourteen chokes are four separate single-core sections, so the Cp · N · √n_c stacking assumption is untested by every measurement here. Any modeled build containing a two-sleeve section rests on it.

Four of fourteen is the hit rate on the full criterion, and every one of the four depends on a five-turn section that has no bench sweep: #43@5T for chokes 1, 2 and 13, #31@5T for choke 14. No sleeve of any mix was ever swept at five turns.

One sweep per choke, no repeats, so the measurement scatter is still unknown. The only pair of sweeps of one assembly, choke 12 and choke 12 re-dressed, changed the dressing on purpose and so measures that rather than the scatter.

The 5-kilohm criterion is a target, not a measured requirement. Nothing here measures common-mode current on an actual antenna.

k rests on a resonance proxy, a smoothed impedance maximum rather than a measured resonance, and mix 75 is the case that proves the proxy can fail.

RG-316 caps the choke near a few hundred watts. It buys the turns these designs need at the cost of power handling, and nothing here weighs that trade.

Chokes 12, 13 and 14 were swept eight and nine days after the other eleven, against the same SJ000046 reference with the fixture untouched. That is defensible because at these impedances the reference barely matters: subtracting it and using none at all agree within 0.3 percent on every band, 453 nH being 81 ohms at 28.4 MHz against 8,000 of winding. It is not the same as having swept them the same day.

What to build and measure

Design by model is finished until the inputs improve. The remaining measurements are the ones that would make a model possible, in the order they pay.

Wind choke 13 a second time and sweep it, changing nothing. This is now the first thing to do. It bounds the scatter that every single-variable comparison above lacks, and without it the 57 ohms between chokes 11 and 12, and the 338 between choke 12 re-dressed and choke 13, cannot be called differences at all. Choke 14 makes the case sharper: it clears dominance across the whole of 10 m by 0.0034 where choke 12 re-dressed failed by 0.002, and nothing here can say those two sit on opposite sides of anything.

Sweep each candidate section alone, in the assembly fixture, at the assembly lead dressing. Six sweeps now: #75@8T, #31@4T, #31@6T, #31@8T, #43@4T, #43@5T, #31@5T. The two five-turn sections come first: all four builds that meet the full criterion depend on one of them and nothing here characterizes either. Then predict a held-out choke and check. This is the one experiment that would turn a measured rule back into a design tool.

Re-dress choke 13. Dressing bought 22 and 52 percent at 10 m on the two assemblies it was tried on, and choke 13 has not had that treatment at its final turn counts. Its worst band is 10 m at 8,587 ohms with R/|Z| 0.782, so there is dominance margin to spend.

Try #43@4T at all. No assembly here uses a four-turn mix-43 section; five is the fewest any of the fourteen was wound with. On the bench that four-turn sleeve is the only section measured whose resistance is still climbing at 28 MHz, which is the direction every one-at-a-time pair points. Whether a fourth turn taken off lifts choke 13 further, or lifts chokes 1 and 2, is untested in both directions.

Sweep a stacked section. It is the largest untested assumption still in the model.

Mix 75 from 100 kHz. It now measures load-bearing at 10 m as well as at the bottom of the range, which no model expected.

Update, 2026-11-22: a coax-wound choke is not a ladder-line balun

A winding made after this article was published and for a different job: a 1:1 current balun to take a doublet’s 300 ohm TV twin lead into a wide-range tuner, twin lead on one end and coax on the other. Three sleeves rather than four, #31@8T + #31@8T + #43@6T, the same RG-316, the same fixture, the same SJ000046 reference the day’s other sweeps use. The file is SJ000060.

It is not one of the fourteen. It was not part of the design search, it has three sections rather than four, and it uses no mix 75. It is here because of what it measures and what that turned out not to be worth.

Three sections in line and spaced apart, the same dress as the four-core assemblies. The pink-taped winding at the edge of the frame is one of those.

It clears 5 kilohms on every band from 80 m through 10 m and fails the dominance half of the criterion on 15, 12 and 10 m. Its worst band in that range is 5,007 ohms at 10 m. What is distinctive is where it turns over: self-resonance at 7.86 MHz is the earliest of any build here, against 9.77 MHz for choke 13 and 19.49 for choke 2 re-dressed, and everything above it is spent on the capacitive side.

The shaded span is where both tests hold: R past 5 kilohms and R above |X|, which is the same thing as R/|Z| at or above 0.707. It runs 2.8 to 21.1 MHz. Self-resonance arrives at 7.9 MHz, very early, and the reactance is capacitive and growing from there until it overtakes the resistance at 21 MHz. The table above gives what the magnitude would have said instead: 8,417 ohm at 10 m against 5,007 of resistance.

Self-resonance lands at 7.86 MHz, against 19.5 MHz for choke 2 re-dressed. Two mix-31 sections at eight turns each is a great deal of inductance, and the R-L-C fit above 9 MHz puts 0.60 pF of stray across it, so the turnover arrives early and the whole upper range sits on the capacitive side of the peak. The big middle is bought with both ends, which is the trade the design rule above predicts and this winding pays in full.

The de-embedding barely matters for this one, which is worth recording because it is the opposite of the single-sleeve case. Subtracting SJ000046 and using no reference at all agree within 0.3 percent on every band: 453 nH is 81 ohms at 28.4 MHz against 8,400 ohms of winding. Dividing the same reference, the wrong convention for this fixture, falls to −765 ohms at 28.4 MHz, which is unphysical. The reading is 5 percent of the fixture ceiling at 10 m, inside the 0 to 9 percent the sweeps above run at, and removing the open shunt raises 10 m from 5,007 to 5,392 ohms without changing a grade.

It is not the balun it was built to be, and the sweep cannot show why

A series-through measurement reads the outside of the shield. The transmitted signal travels inside, and for a coax-wound Guanella that inside path is a 50 ohm transmission line. A 50 ohm line is transparent only when it is terminated in 50 ohms. A doublet on 300 ohm twin lead presents anything from a few ohms to several thousand, mostly reactive, which is the whole reason a wide-range tuner is in the plan.

Twenty-two turns around a 25.91 × 12.80 × 28.60 mm sleeve, plus the inter-core runs and the pigtails, is roughly 2.2 m of coax. In RG-316 at a velocity factor of 0.695 that is not electrically short:

Band 80 40 20 15 10
electrical length 13° 27° 53° 79° 106°
a 300 ohm load arrives as 107 − j138 38 − j88 13 − j36 9 − j9 9 + j14

Past 1.84 m the section is beyond a quarter wave at 10 m, and the transformation runs the wrong way. It drives the tuner toward the low-impedance corner, which is the corner a T-network runs out of range in and gets lossy in before it does.

Then the loss. A 50 ohm section carrying a large mismatch dissipates roughly its matched loss multiplied by (s + 1/s)/2:

SWR on the internal section 20 m 10 m
10:1 0.50 dB, 11% 0.72 dB, 15%
20:1 1.00 dB, 21% 1.43 dB, 28%
50:1 2.49 dB, 44% 3.56 dB, 56%

At 100 watts that is 11 to 56 watts turned to heat inside 2.2 m of thin coax with no thermal mass to put it in. The voltage is the other half: a load of 200 + j2000 at 100 watts stands 1,421 volts RMS across the dielectric, past what RG-316 is rated to hold. At QRP the heating falls by a factor of ten and the voltage by three, so the cable survives, but the impedance transformation does not care what power runs through it.

Watch out. Everything in this subsection is arithmetic on an estimated cable length, not a measurement. The sweep measures the choke; nothing here measures the balun. The test that settles it is a non-inductive 300 ohms across the twin-lead end and an S11 sweep from the coax end: a transparent 1:1 balun reads a flat 300 ohms from 1 to 30 MHz, and where that reading has traveled to by 28 MHz gives the electrical length directly.

The general statement is the part worth keeping. Every grading in this article assumes the choke sits on a 50 ohm line carrying a matched signal, where the length of the wound line is invisible and only Zcm matters. Put the same winding in a high-SWR balanced feed and the wound length becomes the binding constraint instead, and no series-through sweep will ever show it. A coax choke belongs in coax. A ladder-line balun wants its wound line close to the impedance of the line it feeds, or wants to be short enough that the question does not arise.
